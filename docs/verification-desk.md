## Patterns

- **The hard numbers held up.** All 12 sampled claims about scope classes, token lifetimes, user caps, install thresholds and review windows match current primary sources. Most were taken from the right official pages, including recent changelogs (Slack 2025-05-29 and 2026-09-01, HubSpot sunset, Notion 2026-06-08).
- **Over-generalising qualifiers.** The errors that did show up are about scope words, not numbers: "every app must" (LinkedIn says "for certain functionality and products"), "only if" for CASA (Google says "if"), "only through the CLI" (HubSpot restricted the UI), "on/after" vs "after". The agents tend to turn conditional official wording into absolutes.
- **Editorial judgment labelled `sourced`.** Phrases like "approval is unlikely for general developers" (LinkedIn) and "closed" go beyond what the source says. They should be labelled `inferred` or split from the sourced fact.
- **Some labels are too cautious.** Slack `nuances[1]` (existing installs exempt) is labelled `unverified` even though the changelog says so directly. The GitHub research note says no changelog dates the OAuth App default change, but github.blog has one (2026-08-14). The agents sometimes stopped searching before finding the confirming primary source.
- **Thresholds that move.** Slack's Marketplace threshold went 10 → 5 (Aug 2025) → 10 (Jul 2026). The guide has the current value, but this kind of fact goes stale fast and needs a "last checked" date.
